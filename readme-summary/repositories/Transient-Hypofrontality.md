# Transient-Hypofrontality

Summary:

The Transient-Hypofrontality repository focuses on exploring the intersection between psychological flow states and transient hypofrontality—a momentary reduction in cerebral blood flow within the prefrontal cortex. This condition, documented since 1974 via xenon imaging techniques, is associated with various neurological disorders including schizophrenia, ADHD, bipolar disorder, and depression. Neuroimaging studies using PET scans with 18F-FDG tracers have localized hypofrontality to specific regions of the medial, lateral, and orbital prefrontal cortex, suggesting its role in exacerbating negative symptoms in psychiatric conditions.

The repository likely contains software or experimental artifacts designed to model, simulate, or analyze these phenomena, potentially leveraging mathematical frameworks from neuroimaging research. It may include data visualizations, analytical tools, or theoretical models that bridge the gap between psychological flow states and hypofrontality, aiming to deepen understanding of how transient brain state alterations influence mental health outcomes.
