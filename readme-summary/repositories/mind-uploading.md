# mind-uploading

**Summary**

The repository titled *mind-uploading* explores why uploading human consciousness—often framed as transferring one's “self” into a digital substrate—is fundamentally flawed. It argues that identity is not merely a static pattern (as functionalism suggests) but an ongoing, dynamic process governed by the Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework. According to RSVP:

1. **Category Error** – Consciousness is likened to a flame rather than a file; copying its “pattern” does not preserve identity because experience is a continuous occurrence, not a static record.
2. **Branching Trap** – Any scan and reconstruction creates two separate trajectories (original vs. copy), leading to a new entity that merely resembles the original but lacks true continuity of experience.
3. **Entropy & Coherence** – Identity persists only within the Coherence Manifold M, defined by global phase coupling and entropy fields; once λₘₐₓ becomes positive, self‑fragmentation occurs irreversibly.
4. **Acausal Threats** – Concepts like Roko’s Basilisk fail because utility functions aggregate over specific identity classes; a punishment of a digital twin does not affect the current experiential thread.
5. **Gradual Exception** – The only viable path to “digital life” is through gradual substrate replacement (the Ship of Theseus method), preserving continuous causal flow rather than terminating and restarting.

The repository concludes that survival hinges on maintaining this unbroken physical process, not merely replicating information or memories. It emphasizes the philosophical and technical distinction between a copy and genuine continuity of consciousness.

**KEYWORDS:** mind uploading, consciousness transfer, functionalism critique, Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP), identity as dynamical process, category error, branching trap, entropy fields, Coherence Manifold, gradual substrate replacement.
